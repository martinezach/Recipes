## Transfer Buffer (Wet)
The composition of transfer buffer is highly variable and can be significantly optimized empirically for different applications to maximize the efficiency of protein transfer. In different Transfer buffer compositions different biochemically distinct proteins will transfer with varying efficiency.

| Component | Final conc. (1x) | 1L 10x | 2L 10x |
|:----------|:-----------------|:-------|:-------|
| Tris      | 25 mM            | 30.3 g | 60.6 g |
| Glycine   | 192 mM           | 144 g  | 288 g  |
| Alcoholº  | 10-20%           | ø      | ø      |
| SDSºº     | optional         | ø      | ø      |

~~~~~
pH should be in the range of 8.1-8.8, Dont adjust.
~~~~~

+ º Only add alcohol to the final buffer formulation and not to the stock to avoid evaporation. Methanol or ethanol can be effectively used in this range for most proteins, but can be used in concentrations as low as 5% in some cases. In many places the toxicity of methanol requires specialized handling of the resulting waste, so overall its often simpler and more cost effective to use ethanol. As a general use formulation 10% ethanol is very good and cost effective.
+ ºº SDS can be added in low concentrations (0.1%) to aid in the transfer of some hydrophobic or high molecular weight proteins.

## Staining of electrophorized proteins
### Ponceau-S staining
Fast and practical stain, good sanity check to assure succesfull transfer. Compatible with downstream Western blotting. The revesrible nature of the stainig means that staining intensity depends on washing and is thus **not reliable for normalization**.

+ After transfer wash membrane thuroughly under running tap water
+ Add poneau-S stain solution to cover membrane and agitate for 2 min
+ Recover Poceau stain for reuse (can be used many times until it looks nasty)
+ Wash 1-2 times with distilled water. Eyeball the sweetspot between low background and loss of band intensity.
+ Document.
+ Rinse for a while in TBS to destain and equilibrate membrane for downstream blotting.


### India ink staining
### Amido black staining
Permanent staining of proteins **Not compatible with western blot(can interfere with antibody binding)** but can be used after blotting is finished to allow for total protein load normalization.

+ Thuroughly wash membrane in water 3 times.
+ Soak in amido black stain for 1 min.
+ Destain to reduce background with 5% acetic acid 2 times for 1 min each.
+ Thuroughly rinse the membrane with water.
+ Air dry, document, and set for storage.

### TCE staining
Modern "stain-free" commercial kits allow for uv-catalized fluorecent staining of proteins in the PAGE gel. Though commercial kits use more sophisticated reagents, the same effect can be emulated with the addition of Tricloroethanol to the gel formulation. Once run the gel is exposed to 1-2 min uv light in transiluminator to catalize formation of fluorecent complexes. This staining is permanent and can be imaged at this point, and then again after protein transfer to the membrane (both on-membrane and on-gel) allowing for accurate tracing of proein transfer and normalization of Blot data to total protein load per lane. **Compatible with Western Blotting**

### TBE-Buffer
Works better for smaller fragments (<2 kb). Borate can inhibit reactions in downstream processig, so not ideal for cloning purposes. Less prone to overheating that TAE and allows higher voltage (faster) runs. Can be used effectively at 1x or 0.5x dilutions. Stocks any higher than 10x are not recommended due to low solubility of the borate salt. Run at 5-10 V/cm.

# Other solutions for DNA work
## Solvents for DNA
While water is an effective solvent for most applications its often not the optimal solution for specific applications, particularly since distilled and otherwise procesed water can have unknown pH conditions and might make DNA liable to acid hidrolisis.
Troubleshooting:
+ Overdrying of DNA after precipitation can make resuspension more diffficult.
+ Incomplete resusupension could be due to too low solvent volume.
+ Heating solvent to 60º makes resuspension easier

## Nucleic acid/Nuclease decontaminating solution
Used extensively for surface decontamination in RNA work. (By Pipettejockey, Alexander Klenov circa 2017). Alcaline bleach solution efficiently deactivates RNase, while SDS helps with cleaning and eliminating organic load. Comercial detergents like Alconox or Sparkleen can be substituted at 1%. Sodium bicarbonate inhibits the capacity of the bleach to corode different materials.
Can also be used at 0.5x to clean glassware.

| Component           | Final conc. | 1 L    | 100 ml          |
|:--------------------|:------------|:-------|:----------------|
| Bleach (commercial) | 10% (v/v)   | 100 ml | 10 ml           |
| NaOH                | 1 %   (w/v) | 10 g   | 5 ml, 20% stock |
| SDS                 | 0.1% (w/v)  | 1 g    | 1 ml, 10% stock |
| Sodium bicarbonate  | 90 mM       | 7.5 g  | 0.75 g          |
